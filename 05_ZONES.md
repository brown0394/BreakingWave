# Beach Map and Character Pools

## Map Overview

Fog-covered beach. Advance from bottom to top.
5 zones, 3 bunkers, 3 enemy infantry positions, 7–9 landing craft (Decision 048).

```
[High Ground — Breach Objective]
─────────────────────────────────
Zone 4: Defense Line
  - 3 MG Bunkers (left, center, right)
  - Connected by communication trenches
─────────────────────────────────
Zone 3: Upper Beach
  - Enemy infantry: Foxhole (left), Trench line (center), Foxhole (right)
  - Cover: 3 sand dunes, debris pile
─────────────────────────────────
Zone 2: Obstacle Field
  - Czech hedgehogs in 2 staggered rows
  - 1 barbed wire line
  - Partial cover possible
─────────────────────────────────
Zone 1: Waterline (Kill Zone)
  - No cover. Fully exposed.
  - Concentrated fire from all 3 bunkers
─────────────────────────────────
Zone 0: Landing Point
  - 7–9 landing craft ~75 m apart (3 greybox hulls placed today)
─────────────────────────────────
[Sea]
```

## Zone Details

### Zone 0 — Landing (5~15 seconds)
Starts the moment the landing craft ramp drops. Wade from water to beach.
**7–9 craft roughly 75 m apart** across the frontage (Decision 048) — your starting craft
determines your lane up the beach. Craft **sail in, ground, drop the ramp, disgorge ~25–30 men
and back off** (Decision 049); they are a running cycle, not scenery.

### Zone 1 — Waterline, Kill Zone (10~30 seconds)
Zero cover. Crossfire from bunker MGs. Most deaths happen here.
First character (landing craft soldier) runs ten steps and falls here.
Fallen bodies gradually pile up — becoming the only "cover."

### Zone 2 — Obstacle Field (20~45 seconds)
Anti-tank obstacles (Czech hedgehogs) paradoxically provide cover.
Staggered in 2 rows, requiring zigzag movement between them.
Barbed wire impedes movement. Must cut through or go around.
The zone where judgment begins.

### Zone 3 — Upper Beach, Engagement Zone (30~60 seconds)
First direct engagement with enemy infantry. Trading fire from behind sand dunes.
2 enemy foxholes (left, right) and 1 trench line (center).
Allied forces shift from "advancing" to "fighting" here.
**Enemy characters are playable in this zone and Zone 4.**

### Zone 4 — Defense Line (30~90 seconds)
3 concrete MG bunkers, all manned (Decision 033); the center one is larger.
Communication trenches between bunkers allow troop movement.
Breaching here reaches the high ground and ends the game.
Longest and hardest zone.

## Grey-Box Heightmap (tentative)

Generated by `Tools/GenerateBeachHeightmap.ps1` → `SourceAssets/BeachHeightmap_1009.png`.
1009×1009 px, 1 px = 1 m at landscape scale **100/100/200**. Gray value 32768 = Z 0 = waterline,
64 gray steps = 1 m. (Z=200 settled during Step 1: profile-true heights at Z=100 read as flat
in first person. The script's tables are in world meters at this scale.)
Advance direction is +Y (image top = sea). Landscape actor at origin gives these world positions:

|------|-------------|--------|
| Sea floor | 0–250 | -16 → -3 m |
| Zone 0 shallows | 250–310 | -3 → 0 m |
| Zone 1 kill zone | 310–390 | 0 → +4 m, near-flat |
| Zone 2 obstacle field | 390–480 | +4 → +12 m |

| Zone 4 defense line | 580–660 | +26 → +48 m |

| High ground plateau | 730–1008 | +64 m |

Tactical relief baked into the heightmap (tables/constants at the top of the script):

- 3 dunes in Zone 3 at world X ≈ 250/510/770, ~4.4–6 m above local ground — these are
  the "3 sand dunes" cover; they block sightlines from the defense line
- Shingle berm ridge at the Zone 2/3 boundary (Y ≈ 478), up to ~2.8 m with gaps —
  partial cover with exposed crossings
- Bluff and defense line waver ±15 m laterally instead of running straight
- 13 shell craters with raised rims (Craters table), mostly Zone 1 with a few bleeding
  into Zone 0/2 edges. Only the 3 deep ones (X ≈ 150/430/610, 1.6–2.0 m deep + ~0.3–0.6 m rim)
  are usable cover — kill-zone cover stays scarce by design. The ~10 shallow ones
  (0.5–0.9 m) are bombardment dressing, no protection from plunging MG fire

- The zigzag trench channel in Zone 4 — 4 m wide × 1.2 m deep, carved from
  `Tools/TrenchCenterline.json` rather than from a table in the generator. See **The Trench**
  below. Whether the level carries the current PNG is in `02_STATUS.md`

Zone lengths are first guesses. Walk the grey-box (Step 1), then adjust the
Profile table at the top of the script, re-run it, and re-import.

## The Trench

Topology is Decision **051**, the carve-plus-script hybrid is **052**, the shared-file and
contour choices are **056**, and the zigzag, lower-lip depth and open ends are **057**. The route is
authored in the `Route` table of `Tools/GenerateTrenchCenterline.ps1`, which writes
`Tools/TrenchCenterline.json`; `GenerateBeachHeightmap.ps1` runs it before carving. Both consumers
read only the JSON — the heightmap generator to carve the channel, workstream C's placement script
to emit parapet, firing steps and `TrenchNode` actors — so geometry and navigation cannot disagree.
Never edit the JSON by hand.

| Segment | Route (profile metres) | Path length |
|---------|------------------------|-------------|
| Fire trench | x 150 → 870, zigzagging about the contour | 880 m |
| Communication trench ×3 | fire trench → round one side of the bunker → behind it → bluff entry, at x 200 / 510 / 800 | 112 / 114 / 116 m |
| Door stub ×3 | communication trench → bunker rear door | 3.5 m each |
| Sap ×3 | fire trench → the Zone 3 outposts at x 355 / 510 / 655 | 64 / 60 / 74 m |

- **Zigzag**: every span between two labelled route points that is at least 24 m long becomes legs
  of ~10 m — 8 m of advance, 6 m of swing. The swing is wider than the 4 m channel, so a corner hides
  the next leg. Labelled points stay on the route with no offset.
- **The fire trench zigzags about the contour, not a constant row.** Zone 4 is warped laterally by
  `BluffWaviness(x) × 15 m`, so each fire-trench route row is `600 − BluffWaviness(x) × 15` — the
  line bows inland by up to 22 m through the middle of the frontage. **Re-derive every fire-trench
  and bluff-entry row if the `Profile` or `BluffWaviness` in the generator ever changes.**
- **Depth is measured from the lower lip** at the nearest centreline point, with the lips sampled
  with noise, and the floor is one height across the channel. On the defense line's 28% slope the
  lower lip is the seaward one, so the front wall is the full depth and the rear wall is taller.
- **Open ends surface**: bluff entries, door stubs and sap ends blend to untouched ground over the
  last ~8 m. Only the fire trench's two line ends are dead ends.

Measured on the generated heightmap, 2026-10-10:

- Front wall median **1.08 m**, rear wall median **2.29 m**, 95th percentile **2.47 m** (both
  measured 0.5–1 m past the lip). The rear wall is out of reach without the climb (Decision 058)
- Fire-trench floor **27.9–30.5 m** — the zigzag crosses the contour ±3 m and the floor follows the
  real ground. Steepest along-floor grade **0.20**
- Every open end rises at most **0.6 m per metre** into the ground beyond it; door stubs meet the
  ground within **0.12 m** of the threshold
- Communication-trench centrelines stay **4 m** clear of the bunker footprints

## Character Pool Structure

### Allied Character Pool
Placed in Zones 0~2. People advancing up the beach.
2~3 candidates per zone. Random selection from nearby candidates based on death zone.
If all candidates in a zone are dead, expand to adjacent zone candidates.

> **What is built (Decisions 038, 041)**: the next character is an anonymous live ally within
> **~35 m (fog range) of where you fell**, and no more than 20 m further forward — or a man
> manufactured at the edge of that disc if nobody is in it. Candidates are ally-sim slots, not
> authored characters; the authored registry above waits on narrative volume.

### German Character Pool
Placed in Zones 3~4. People looking down at the beach.
Trench infantry, foxhole riflemen, bunker MG gunners — ordinary soldiers, no officer story.
If an Allied character's death narrative mentions "the person who shot me,"
the next character can be German.
